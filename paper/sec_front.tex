\section{Introduction}

Phishing email is still one of the most common ways into an organization. The
attacker does not break any technical control. They write a message that looks
like it came from a bank, a colleague or a delivery company, and the reader
hands over the password. Generative AI made this cheaper. Heiding et
al.~\cite{heiding} showed that phishing written by GPT-4 already gets click
rates close to phishing written by human experts.

The defense side has moved to language models too, and the published numbers
look excellent. ChatSpamDetector reports 99.70\% accuracy with
GPT-4~\cite{koide}. A fine-tuned RoBERTa reaches 98.45\%~\cite{uddin}. A five
agent LLM system reaches 97.89\%~\cite{multiphish}. When we reproduced the
setting with a plain TF-IDF and Logistic Regression model, we also got 97 to
99\% accuracy. A method older than deep learning matching GPT-4 is a warning
sign. It suggests the benchmark, not the model, is doing the work.

Two things make these numbers hard to trust. First, most studies train and
test inside a single corpus, while a real mail server sees mail that looks
nothing like any training set. Second, the public corpora are not
independent. Popular datasets such as the Kaggle phishing email set are
aggregates built from older collections, so the same email can sit on both
sides of a train and test split, even when the two sides come from
``different'' datasets. A third, practical point is cost. For a university or
a small company, the price of running a detector matters as much as its
accuracy, yet none of the LLM papers we reviewed report it.

This paper asks a simple question. If we remove the leaked emails and always
test on a corpus the model has never seen, how good are cheap detectors, and
what do they cost? Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item A measurement of email overlap between nine public phishing and spam
sources at three levels of strictness. We show that exact matching, the check
most people would run, misses almost all of it.
\item A leave-one-corpus-out benchmark over six corpora in which every
training email that has a near duplicate in the test corpus is removed, and a
measurement of how much leaked emails inflate the score.
\item A comparison of classical models, a fine-tuned DistilBERT and six small
open LLMs (1B to 14B parameters) on the same emails, including AI-written
phishing, with the measured dollar cost of every LLM call.
\end{enumerate}

The whole study cost under one US dollar in API fees and ran on one 8\,GB
laptop. Code, results and a notebook are public.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/Jahid15/phishing-llm-cross-corpus}}

\section{Related Work}

\textit{LLM detectors.} Koide et al.~\cite{koide} prompt GPT-4 with a chain of
thought and parse the verdict. Heiding et al.~\cite{heiding} test GPT-4,
Claude, Bard and LLaMA as judges of suspicious mail. Lin et
al.~\cite{lin} show that 3B models perform poorly out of the box but improve
with prompt engineering, LoRA fine-tuning on explanations from a paid
GPT-4o-mini teacher, and ensembling. They list the missing cost analysis as a
limitation. MultiPhishGuard~\cite{multiphish} combines five cooperating agents.
Phishsense-1B~\cite{phishsense} is a LoRA-tuned 1B model that reports 97.5\%
accuracy on its own data and 70\% on a separate real-world set, the clearest
sign in the literature that in-corpus scores do not transfer.

\textit{Encoders and explanations.} Uddin et al.~\cite{uddin} fine-tune RoBERTa
on the Kaggle set and add LIME based explanations. Kuikel et
al.~\cite{kuikel} find that the most accurate models are not the ones with the
most faithful explanations. Alhuzali et al.~\cite{alhuzali} compare many
classical and deep models on several public datasets.

\textit{Cross-dataset testing.} The closest work to ours is
E-PhishGen~\cite{ephishgen}. The authors reassess eight legacy corpora, show
that a Logistic Regression model trained on CEAS falls from 0.98 F1 to 0.27 on
average on other corpora, and release E-PhishLLM, a set of emails generated
with GPT-4o-mini. We use E-PhishLLM as our AI-phishing test. Our work differs in
three ways: we measure and remove near-duplicate leakage between corpora before
testing, we include the Kaggle aggregate that much of the LLM literature uses,
and we report measured cost next to accuracy.

\textit{Leakage in ML.} Arp et al.~\cite{arp} list data snooping and sampling
bias among the most common pitfalls of machine learning in security. Kapoor and
Narayanan~\cite{kapoor} trace many published results in other fields to
leakage between training and test data. Our overlap study is a concrete case of
this problem in phishing detection.

\section{Data}

We use nine sources (Table~\ref{tab:data}). Six contain both classes and serve
for training and testing: SpamAssassin, CEAS-08, TREC-07, Ling-Spam and Enron
from the curated collection of Champa et al.~\cite{champa}, and the Kaggle
phishing email set used by~\cite{uddin}. Three are test-only. Nazario contains
real credential phishing and Nigerian contains advance-fee fraud, both with no
legitimate class. E-PhishLLM~\cite{ephishgen} contains phishing and legitimate
emails written by GPT-4o-mini. We keep its English part.

For every source the model input is the subject and the body joined by a new
line. Label 1 means phishing or spam and label 0 means legitimate. We drop
bodies shorter than 20 characters and exact duplicates inside a corpus. Corpora
larger than 10{,}000 emails are reduced to a stratified sample of 10{,}000
(seed 42) so that the three largest ones do not dominate training. From each
source we also draw a fixed evaluation subset of 300 emails, balanced where
both classes exist (150 for the one-class sets). All models are scored on these
same emails, which makes the LLM and non-LLM numbers directly comparable.

The positive class is not the same across corpora. SpamAssassin, CEAS, TREC and
Ling mark ordinary spam as positive, while Nazario is pure phishing. We keep
the labels as published and phrase the LLM prompt to match (``phishing, scam or
spam''), and we report recall on Nazario and Nigerian separately.
